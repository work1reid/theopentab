Plenty of people call themselves songwriters. Cameron Sharp has a higher bar — and he's honest that clearing it is brutal.

## Anyone can; few can well

"Everyone can — anyone can just say 'I'm a songwriter,'" he says. "And then I hear their music and think, well, that's crap." The distinction he draws is between *writing a song* and *writing a good one*:

> "To be a good songwriter, you need life experience. You need to read. You need to know what's going on in the world. You need to be emotionally intelligent. You need to understand people. You can't just grab a guitar, lay down some chords and put words to it and think, oh, I'm a songwriter."

It's the same theme that runs through everything Cameron believes: the surface is easy, the substance is earned.

## The line that reframed it

He cites the lead singer of one of his favourite bands, Mumford & Sons, on what songwriting should feel like:

> "Songwriting should be the hardest thing you ever do."

Cameron agrees, with a confession most musicians won't make: he doesn't actually *enjoy* songwriting. "Once you get a good song, then it's fun." But getting there means digging "deep into what you're really feeling at that time, which is not easy." The reward is a specific, unmistakable feeling — "you know it's good, you just know" — but you have to mine for it.

## Hearing his own growth

The proof, for Cameron, is in his own catalogue. Listening back to his first single recently, he could hear the gap: "I can see how a listener would be thinking, 'what's he singing about?'" Back then, he says, "you know what you want to say, but you don't know how to say it." Too focused on making it *sound* nice rather than letting what's real come out.

Now? "My songs are actually making sense." He's in a groove where the craft and the feeling finally line up — which is exactly what years of doing the hard thing buys you.

The takeaway for any writer: if it feels too easy, you're probably still on the surface. The good stuff is on the other side of the hard part.
