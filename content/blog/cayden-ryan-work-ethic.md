People look at Cayden Ryan now — steady job in the mines, nice car, a plan — and assume it came easy. He's the first to push back. "I think you might see me as more successful than I see myself," he says. And the foundation of all of it was laid years ago, on a schedule almost nobody would volunteer for.

## The four-job-shaped teenager

Before the mines, there was McDonald's. For four years. While he was still at school.

Picture the week: school Monday to Friday, then straight to Macca's from 4:30pm to midnight, every weeknight. Then, when KFC opened up locally, he took weekend day shifts there too. So at one point a teenage Cayden was effectively running school full-time *and* a job full-time, with a second job stacked on the weekends.

"I had money in my account at that point," he remembers, "but I still couldn't see *this*" — meaning the life and the work he'd eventually grow into. The money wasn't the lesson. The habit was.

## Why it matters more than talent

What that grind built wasn't a bank balance — it was a default setting. By the time Cayden reached the mines, showing up and grafting wasn't a decision he had to make each day; it was just who he was.

And in mining, that's the whole entry ticket. He's blunt about it:

> "You need a good work ethic. You've got to prove it."

On site you're surrounded by people who've put in twenty years of hard work and have earned the right to ease off a little. A newcomer who arrives with the wrong mentality — "coming in thinking you know everything when you don't" — gets found out fast. "I've seen people last a week," Cayden says.

## The quiet point underneath

There's something worth sitting with here. The work ethic that makes Cayden valuable now wasn't handed to him and wasn't a personality trait he was born with. It was *built*, shift by shift, when he was young and tired and could easily have done less.

It's also why he bristles at the assumption that things were given to him — but that's a story for [another part of the conversation](/blog/cayden-ryan-mines-money-family). For now, the takeaway is simple and a little old-fashioned: the habit you build early becomes the advantage you carry for life.
