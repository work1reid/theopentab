Long before two million views, Cameron Sharp was "a short little fat kid" — his words — standing nervous on a stage at the Forbes Town Hall.

## The buzz that started it all

He was 10, entering the school talent quest, playing Coldplay's "Yellow." His voice was shaky. He got through it, in key, but rattled. What he remembers isn't the performance — it's looking out and seeing the teachers smiling back.

"I got a buzz off the crowd, and I just was like, I want more of that."

That's the moment the whole thing switched on. He's clear about how contingent it was, too: "If you hadn't performed well, I don't think you would have" kept going. One good night at 10 lit the fuse.

## Tamworth at 14

Four years later, the fuse had become a fire. Cameron and his schoolmate Kent took their act busking to the Tamworth Country Music Festival — the country-music capital of Australia. The format was almost like a talent show on the street: scouts walked the festival picking the best buskers, the top five earning a slot on the main stage.

Cameron and Kent got picked. They played to **around 4,000 people**, covering Mumford & Sons' "I Will Wait" and Green Day's "Good Riddance (Time of Your Life)."

He was 14. Still at school, still playing sport, still figuring out who he was. "When you're a kid starting out, you just don't know who you are. So you're just trying to be like your heroes" — which for him meant Mumford & Sons, played "to death" because they always got a reaction.

## The trajectory, seen from outside

What's striking is that Cameron had never really framed his own come-up until it was laid out for him: first performance at 10, main stage at 14. "I've never thought of it like that," he admitted. "That's crazy, actually."

It's a common thing with people in the middle of the grind — you don't see how far you've come until someone steps back and shows you. "I don't see the growth, because you don't see how far you've come until you actually step back and look."

From a shaky talent quest to 4,000 people in four years — and that was just the beginning of the story.
