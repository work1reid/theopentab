If you had to reduce Cameron Sharp to one phrase, it'd be the one he keeps returning to: *hard work beats talent.*

It isn't a slogan he picked up. It's the conclusion of a hard few years.

## Outclassed at music college

When Cameron left school for music college, he didn't feel like he belonged. "Everyone around you was just so much more talented naturally," he says. The people beside him seemed to have it all handed to them — the ear, the voice, the ease. He didn't. "I knew I had something, but I just wasn't confident in it."

So he made a decision that defined everything after: if talent wasn't going to carry him, work would. He pulled out of uni early — "I just wasn't getting anything out of it" — and threw himself into the only thing he believed would close the gap: gigging.

## Learning the unglamorous skills

The grind was specific and unsexy. Learning "how to sing in a pub for four hours straight without losing your voice." Playing to empty rooms — "I play to no one probably more than I play to people." Going into bars off his own back to ask for gigs instead of waiting to be booked.

He points to Ed Sheeran as proof of concept: old videos of Sheeran singing badly, voice cracking, before years of consistent work compounded into mastery. "That's how it is. You just keep compounding the talent."

## The advice, unfiltered

Ask Cameron what he'd tell a 10-year-old who wants to be a musician and the answer is refreshingly free of mysticism:

> "Just go for it. Know that you are going to have to put in a lot of work to get better. Accept that. Don't fight it. You're not the best now — you never will be the best. But love what you do."

That last line matters. Cameron says he only started genuinely *loving* music about three years ago, at 27 — after years of comparing himself to others and grinding without joy. The shift wasn't getting better; it was falling in love with the work itself.

He pairs it with one more rule for anyone coming up: don't let your ego run the show, and don't be rude. "You're never going to get anywhere by being rude to people or letting your ego take over." Be the quiet achiever, keep grinding, stay kind.

It's not a shortcut. That's exactly the point.
